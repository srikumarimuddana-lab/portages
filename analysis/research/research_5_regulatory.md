## 1-Page Compliance Checklist for Launch (Regina, MVP = listings + chat + bookings + alerts + AVM + ID-check)

**Before launch — must do**
- [ ] **Stay outside "trading in real estate."** Do not: advise on price, negotiate, draft/convey offers, hold deposits, show property, or collect any fee that looks like compensation for a trade done "on behalf of" a seller/buyer. Owners self-serve everything. Get a legal opinion memo mapping each product feature to *The Real Estate Act* (SK) s.2 "trade" + s.3 exemptions. (§1) **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW**
- [ ] **Written feature guardrail doc** for staff/support: no pricing advice, no offer help, no negotiation relay beyond neutral message transport. (§1)
- [ ] **Trademark hygiene:** never use REALTOR®, MLS®, or Multiple Listing Service® in branding, product names, SEO copy, or as generic nouns ("list on our MLS" is prohibited). (§3)
- [ ] **CASL consent stack:** express opt-in checkboxes (unchecked by default) for saved-search alerts and marketing email/SMS; sender ID + unsubscribe in every message; log consents. (§5)
- [ ] **PIPEDA program:** privacy policy, purpose-limited ID verification (verify, don't hoard — delete/redact ID images after verification per OPC guidance), retention schedule, breach-response plan, DPAs with the ID-verification vendor. (§5)
- [ ] **AVM & AI-description disclaimers:** "estimate, not an appraisal" + methodology/limits; human owner review + attestation of AI-drafted listing text; accuracy claims substantiated (Competition Act deceptive-marketing exposure is now severe). (§8)
- [ ] **"Zero-fee" claims audited** against drip-pricing rules — if anything is ever charged, price it up front. (§8)
- [ ] **Incorporate + GST registration** (>$30k revenue), **SK PST review** of fee characterization, **City of Regina licence/zoning check**. (§10) **UNVERIFIED items flagged**
- [ ] **Insurance:** tech E&O incl. media liability, cyber, CGL, D&O. (§10)

**Do NOT do at launch (each triggers a regime)**
- ✗ Hold buyer deposits or rent money → real-estate trust rules and/or FINTRAC MSB + Bank of Canada RPAA registration (§4)
- ✗ Charge tenants application/screening fees → SK RTA prohibition on application charges (landlord-side; platform work-around is legally fragile) (§6)
- ✗ Provide filled-in offer/lease drafting help → unauthorized practice of law + trading (§1, §7)
- ✗ Display Realtor.ca/MLS® data or scrape it (§3)
- ✗ Say pros are "vetted & insured" without a documented, refreshed verification file per pro (§9)

**Phase-2 triggers to diarize**
- Rent payments / deposits → FINTRAC MSB registration + RPAA (Bank of Canada) + possibly SK property-management brokerage registration (§1, §4)
- Tenant screening product → credit-reporting consent flows, SK Human Rights Code screening limits (§6)
- Mortgage/insurance referrals → referral-fee rules; mortgage sector is now FINTRAC-covered; insurance referrals need licensing analysis (§4, §10)
- Facilitating paid home-services jobs or rentals with payment → CRA digital-platform seller reporting + SK PST marketplace-facilitator analysis (§9, §10)

## 1. Saskatchewan real-estate licensing — *The Real Estate Act* & SREC

**Risk: HIGH if misdesigned / LOW-MEDIUM for a true advertising platform. What to do: build the product to stay outside "trade… on behalf of another for compensation," get a feature-by-feature legal opinion, and monitor SREC bulletins.**

- Governing law: **The Real Estate Act, SS 1995, c R-1.3** (in force Jan 1, 1997; replaced the Real Estate Brokers Act, 1987), administered by the **Saskatchewan Real Estate Commission (SREC)** under delegation, with the **Financial and Consumer Affairs Authority (FCAA)** as umbrella regulator. Sources: https://www.canlii.org/en/sk/laws/stat/ss-1995-c-r-1.3/latest/ss-1995-c-r-1.3.html ; https://fcaa.gov.sk.ca/regulated-businesses-persons/persons/real-estate-agents ; https://www.saskatchewan.ca/government/news-and-media/1995/march/13/new-real-estate-act-introduced (all accessed 2026-08-28).
- **Definition of "trade" (s.2)** — per the Act's text as excerpted: *"'trade' means one or more of the following: (i) a disposition of, an acquisition of or a transaction in real estate by sale, purchase, exchange, option, lease, or otherwise; (ii) an offering, advertisement, listing or showing of real estate for sale, purchase, exchange, option, lease or otherwise…"* (definition continues; counsel should pull the remaining limbs — they typically cover negotiation, collection of rents/property management, and acts in furtherance). Source: https://www.canlii.org/en/sk/laws/stat/ss-1995-c-r-1.3/latest/ss-1995-c-r-1.3.html (accessed 2026-08-28). **Full limb list NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW.**
- **The compensation/agency nexus:** a **"brokerage"** is a registrant that, *"for compensation, gain or hope of reward for the brokerage or any other person, either alone or through its broker… or salesperson trades in real estate located in Saskatchewan,"* and *"no person shall trade in real estate for or on behalf of a brokerage unless the person is registered."* Source: same Act via https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/813/formats/1263/download (accessed 2026-08-28). The registration trigger is trading **for another and for compensation** — advertising your *own* property, or a platform selling *advertising space* rather than trading services, sits outside this structure (see DuProprio litigation, §2). **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW** for Portage's exact feature set.
- **Exemptions — s.3(1):** *"Subject to subsection (2) and section 74, this Act does not apply to: (a) a trade in real estate by an owner of the real estate or a person who has a material interest in the real estate; (b) a trade in real estate on behalf of another person and for that person's account where: (i) no commission or remuneration is paid with respect to the trade; and (ii) the transaction is not made in the course of continued and successive transactions of a like character."* Source: https://www.canlii.org/en/sk/laws/stat/ss-1995-c-r-1.3/latest/ss-1995-c-r-1.3.html (accessed 2026-08-28). Note (b)(ii): repeated, business-like facilitation for others is expressly outside the free-help exemption — a platform must therefore rely on **not trading at all** (being an advertising medium), not on s.3(1)(b). The carve-out "subject to… section 74" was not verifiable in research (**NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW** — confirm what s.74 preserves, likely advertising/enforcement provisions).
- **No express "classifieds/publisher" exemption was located** in the SK Act or Regulations (the Regulations' exemptions found were narrow: small time-share owners, registrant-owned property management with disclosure, Lloydminster/Flin Flon office rules). Sources: https://www.canlii.org/en/sk/laws/regu/rrs-c-r-1.3-reg-1/latest/rrs-c-r-1.3-reg-1.html ; https://publications.saskatchewan.ca//api/v1/products/1383/formats/2403/download (accessed 2026-08-28). Contrast: newspapers/Kijiji-type media have never been treated as trading because they merely sell ad space to owners (owner-exempt trades) — but in SK this rests on the statutory structure and case-law logic, not an explicit classifieds clause. **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW.**
- **"Advertisement"** is defined broadly to include representations "with respect to trading in real estate" in newspapers, flyers, signs, "television, radio or other electronic means" distributed in Saskatchewan — i.e., the Act's advertising rules reach online media when the advertiser is a registrant/trader. Source: https://www.canlii.org/en/sk/laws/stat/ss-1995-c-r-1.3/latest/ss-1995-c-r-1.3.html (accessed 2026-08-28).
- **What crosses the line (researcher's synthesis of the definition + regulator practice; NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW):**
  - *Advising on price / producing a "recommended list price" for a specific seller* → looks like a trading service; a clearly disclaimed, automated AVM shown to everyone is more defensible than bespoke pricing help (§8).
  - *Drafting or transmitting offers, negotiating terms, counselling on counters* → trading (and unauthorized practice of law, §7).
  - *Holding deposits* → registrants must run trust accounts under the Act; an unregistered platform holding transaction money has no lawful home for it and invites both SREC and FINTRAC problems (§4). Avoid entirely.
  - *Arranging viewings*: pure self-serve calendar tooling the owner controls ≈ software; platform staff scheduling/showing on sellers' behalf ≈ "showing of real estate," a trade limb. Keep it tool-like.
  - *Collecting rents / managing rentals for landlords* → property management is registrable activity in SK (SREC licenses a Property Management stream). Source: https://www.sauder.ubc.ca/programs/real-estate/licensing-registration-courses/saskatchewan-registration-courses (accessed 2026-08-28).
- **Enforcement exists:** SREC publishes discipline/consent orders (e.g., files #2022-60, #2023-47, #2024-43, #2024-68), so this is an actively policed regime. Source index: https://www.srec.ca/wp-content/uploads/PDF/DCNS/2024-43.pdf (accessed 2026-08-28; contents not reviewed — **UNVERIFIED** as to subject matter).
- **Modernization status:** the Act was substantially amended effective **May 2020** ("multi-year project… to facilitate the use of modern technologies in real estate transactions" — SREC Information Bulletin, July 2020: https://srec.ca/wp-content/uploads/PDF/IB/IB_AmendmentsRealEstateAct.pdf), with further amendments through 2024. **No replacement "new Real Estate Act" for 2024-2026 was found** in public sources as of 2026-08-28 (searched saskatchewan.ca, FCAA, SREC). Treat "no new Act pending" as **UNVERIFIED** — have counsel check current bills each session.

## 2. Precedents — how no-commission platforms operate

**Risk: LOW (favourable precedent) if Portage mirrors the "advertising medium" model. What to do: choose the DuProprio/Kijiji lane (pure media) or the Bōde lane (own a licensed brokerage) — not a hybrid.**

- **DuProprio (Québec):** sued/prosecuted by the broker regulator **OACIQ** starting 2009 for alleged unlicensed brokerage. Outcomes: **acquitted of six charges** (Court of Québec, Nov 2011; upheld by Québec Superior Court, July 19, 2013); and in **April 2020** the Superior Court again held **DuProprio "does not act as an intermediary" in transactions** and rejected OACIQ's arguments; DuProprio counts four wins across three proceedings. Sources: https://duproprio.com/blog/real-estate-news/duproprio-celebrates-two-wins-against-the-real-estate-brokerage-industry-367 ; https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/lawsuit-against-duproprio-inc---mindful-of-its-impact-on-public-protection-oaciq-appeals-court-of-quebec-decision-509435481.html (OACIQ appealed the Court of Québec decision — final appellate disposition **UNVERIFIED**); Québec's 2018 Bill 5 amendments to the Real Estate Brokerage Act also clarified the FSBO-assistance space: https://duproprio.com/blog/real-estate-news/bill-5-real-estate-brokerage-act-705 (all accessed 2026-08-28). **Take-away:** Canadian courts have accepted that a paid FSBO listing/marketing platform that does not intermediate is not brokering — the strongest precedent in Portage's favour, though it construes Québec's statute, not Saskatchewan's.
- **Bōde (Calgary; operates in AB, BC, ON and SK incl. Regina/Saskatoon):** the opposite architecture — Bōde **is a licensed real-estate brokerage** (RECA-regulated in Alberta) so it can put self-represented sellers on MLS®, run offer flows with standard provincial contracts, and charge $949 flat or 1% capped at $10k on sale. Sources: https://bode.ca/company-overview ; https://home.bode.ca/blog/sell-your-house-without-a-realtor-in-alberta/ ; https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/innovative-real-estate-startup-bdes-well-for-alberta-home-buyers-and-sellers-300916195.html (accessed 2026-08-28). If Portage ever wants MLS® exposure or offer management, the proven route is holding (or partnering with) a SK brokerage registration.
- **PropertyGuys.com:** private-sale *marketing franchise* (Moncton, NB) — sells marketing packages and connects sellers to licensed/professional partners for the regulated pieces; not itself a brokerage. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PropertyGuys.com (accessed 2026-08-28).
- **ComFree:** operates a **"mere posting"** model — flat fee (~$997) that includes an MLS® mere posting placed through a licensed brokerage arm, with the seller otherwise unrepresented. Sources: https://comfree.com/ ; https://realestatemagazine.ca/comfree-resurfaces-in-the-fsbo-market/ (accessed 2026-08-28).
- **Kijiji / Facebook Marketplace:** operate as general classified-advertising media carrying owner-posted real-estate ads without brokerage licences; no Canadian regulator action against them for unlicensed trading was found in research (**UNVERIFIED** — absence of evidence). Their legal logic matches DuProprio's: the *owner* is the exempt trader; the platform sells ad space.

## 3. CREA trademarks, MLS® data, and the Competition Bureau

**Risk: MEDIUM (trademark/copy discipline is easy to get wrong; data access is simply unavailable to non-members). What to do: scrub marks from all copy/SEO; don't touch Realtor.ca data; watch the live Bureau investigation — it may open doors for alternative platforms.**

- **Trademarks:** CREA owns **REALTOR®, MLS®, and Multiple Listing Service®**. They identify services of CREA members: "REALTOR® must not be used as a synonym for real estate agent," and "the MLS® mark can never be used as part of a business name, trade name or in any corporate branding." A non-member platform should avoid the marks except truthful nominative references (with ® and CREA attribution), and must not imply MLS® access or REALTOR® involvement. Sources: https://www.crea.ca/standards-programs/trademark-protection-competition/ ; https://www.crea.ca/cafe/how-to-properly-use-the-mls-trademark/ ; CREA Trademark Manual: https://fullserveweb.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/CREATrademarkManual.pdf (accessed 2026-08-28).
- **MLS data / DDF®:** CREA's **Data Distribution Facility** (Policy & Rules revised **January 2024**: https://www.crea.ca/files/technology/english/DDFR-Policy-and-Rules-February-2024-ENG.pdf) distributes listings **at members' direction**: member-website feeds, a National Shared Pool among participating members, and destination feeds; **technology providers must sign a Data Access Agreement with CREA** before pulling data on members' behalf. Sources: above PDF; https://realtyna.com/blog/data-distribution-facility-crea-ddf-explained/ (accessed 2026-08-28). Practical effect: **a non-member marketplace cannot obtain a Realtor.ca/MLS® feed on its own**; scraping Realtor.ca would breach its terms of use (**UNVERIFIED** — terms not directly reviewed; treat as prohibited).
- **Competition Bureau v. CREA (2010 consent agreement, "mere postings"):** CREA agreed **not to adopt, maintain or enforce rules that deny members the ability to offer mere postings** ("a listing… in respect of which the Member has chosen or agreed not to provide services to the Seller other than submitting the listing for posting"), nor to discriminate against them; term **10 years** (expired Oct 2010 + 10 = Oct 2020, though CREA rules have retained the concept). Sources: consent agreement text: https://www.blaney.com/sites/default/files/Agreement-CREA_HTG_dec2010.pdf ; https://mbrealestate.ca/law-policy/policies-legislation/notices/crea-consent.html (accessed 2026-08-28).
- **Commissioner v. TREB (sold/VOW data):** Competition Tribunal found TREB **abused dominance** by restricting members' use/display of MLS data (incl. sold prices) on Virtual Office Websites; upheld by the Federal Court of Appeal (2017); **SCC denied leave Aug 23, 2018**, ending the 7-year case; TREB then opened the data to member VOWs. Sources: https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2018/08/supreme-court-will-not-hear-toronto-real-estate-boards-appeal.html ; https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/publications/end-7-year-saga-supreme-court-canada-refuses-leave-appeal-abuse-case-against-toronto-real-estate-board (accessed 2026-08-28). Precedent: competition law can pry open MLS data restrictions — but the beneficiaries were *members'* websites, not outside platforms.
- **Live matters (2024-2026):** the Bureau obtained a **Federal Court order in October 2024** compelling CREA records in an investigation of **CREA's commission rules and the REALTOR® Cooperation Policy** — expressly probing whether the policy "makes it more difficult for alternative listing services to compete" (https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2024/10/competition-bureau-advances-investigation-into-the-canadian-real-estate-associations-policies.html); a **second court order (May 2025)** advanced a parallel probe of the **Québec** market (https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2025/05/the-competition-bureau-obtains-a-second-court-order-to-advance-an-investigation-of-competition-in-the-quebec-real-estate-services-market.html); and in **January 2026** the investigation **expanded to Greater Vancouver REALTORS®** (https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2026/01/competition-bureau-expands-its-investigation-of-real-estate-commission-rules-to-include-greater-vancouver-realtors.html) (all accessed 2026-08-28; no findings of wrongdoing yet). Founder angle: Portage is exactly the "alternative listing service" the Bureau worries is being foreclosed — worth a submission if consultations reopen.

## 4. FINTRAC / PCMLTFA (anti-money-laundering)

**Risk: LOW at launch / HIGH the day money is touched. What to do: nothing required for a pure listings platform; a written "no funds handling" policy; full MSB+RPAA workstream before any deposits/rent product.**

- **Who is covered in real estate:** *"real estate brokers or sales representatives"* — defined as persons/entities **"authorized under provincial legislation to act as an agent or mandatary for purchasers or vendors"** — and **real estate developers** are FINTRAC reporting entities (compliance program, KYC/client ID, receipt-of-funds and suspicious/large-cash/terrorist-property reporting). Source: https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/re-ed/real-eng (accessed 2026-08-28). A platform that is **not** provincially authorized to act as agent and does not build/sell new homes is **not** an enumerated reporting entity → **no PCMLTFA obligations for the MVP** (**NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW** to confirm no other category catches specific features). Unrepresented/FSBO deals are a known AML blind spot regulators have flagged — expect policy pressure over time.
- **If Portage becomes a licensed brokerage** (Bōde model), the full real-estate reporting-entity regime attaches. Sector guidance: https://www.reca.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/FINTRAC_Summary_for_Real_Estate_Sector.pdf (accessed 2026-08-28).
- **Deposits / rent payments:** FINTRAC's published position is that **"invoice payment services"** — acting as an intermediary between payer and payee for payments including **"mortgage and rent"** — constitute **money-services-business (MSB) activity** requiring FINTRAC registration and a compliance program (subject to limited exclusions, e.g., agents of the payee / mere technology providers — fact-specific). Sources: https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/notices-avis/2022-04-27-eng ; https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/notices-avis/2022-07-21-eng ; https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/fintrac-issues-clarification-regarding-which-payment-service-providers-need-register-msbs (accessed 2026-08-28). Additionally, payment service providers must **register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA)** before performing retail payment activities (registration regime live since Nov 2024; supervision in force since Sept 2025 — dates **UNVERIFIED**, confirm). Source: https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/msb-esm/questions/2-eng (accessed 2026-08-28). **Design alternative:** use a licensed PSP/trust structure (lawyer's trust account for deposits; a registered PSP for rent) so obligations sit with the licensed party — **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW**.
- **Mortgage referrals (later):** mortgage **brokers, lenders and administrators became PCMLTFA reporting entities effective Oct 11, 2024** (widely reported; e.g., Osler sector summary: https://www.osler.com/osler/media/Osler/reports/anti-money-laundering/Anti-money-laundering-in-Canada-Real-estate-brokers-sales-representatives-and-developers.pdf — **UNVERIFIED as to exact date**; confirm). A pure referrer sending leads to a licensed SK mortgage brokerage (FCAA-licensed under *The Mortgage Brokerages and Mortgage Administrators Act*) is generally outside FINTRAC, but referral-fee disclosure rules apply to the licensee — **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW**.

## 5. Privacy (PIPEDA) & anti-spam (CASL)

**Risk: MEDIUM-HIGH (ID documents are the crown-jewels risk; CASL fines are existential-sized). What to do: minimize ID retention, vendor-diligence the verifier, and run an express-consent CASL stack from day one.**

- **Applicable law:** Saskatchewan has **no private-sector privacy statute**, so **PIPEDA** governs Portage's commercial handling of personal information in SK (SK's FOIP/LA FOIP/HIPA cover public bodies and health custodians). PIPEDA applies to landlord/tenant commercial contexts too: https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/landlords-and-tenants/privacy-in-the-landlord-and-tenant-relationship/ (accessed 2026-08-28). (Federal privacy reform died with Bill C-27 — see §8.)
- **ID-document verification (owners):** OPC guidance: you **may view** government ID to confirm identity, but **"making a copy of such a sensitive document should not be a standard operating practice,"** and an OPC finding held **indefinite retention of driver's-licence numbers violated PIPEDA**; retention must be purpose-limited with scheduled destruction ("no one-size-fits-all" period — document yours). Sources: https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/identities/identification-and-authentication/auth_061013/ ; https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/landlords-and-tenants/privacy-in-the-landlord-and-tenant-relationship/ ; https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2023/03/less-is-more-data-minimization-and-privacy-cyber-risk-management (accessed 2026-08-28). **Design pattern:** verify-then-delete (store a pass/fail + audit hash, not the ID image); if the vendor uses **face-matching/biometrics**, treat as highly sensitive requiring express consent and heightened safeguards — OPC has issued biometrics guidance for businesses (**final-version status UNVERIFIED**; counsel to confirm current text). Mandatory **breach reporting** (real risk of significant harm) applies under PIPEDA (in force since Nov 2018).
- **Fraud note:** title/impersonation fraud is the very risk ID-checking addresses — keep the control, but engineer it so the platform never becomes a honeypot of Regina homeowners' passports.
- **CASL (email + SMS alerts, marketing):** consent is **express** (proactive opt-in — pre-checked boxes are non-compliant) or **implied** (existing business relationship — generally **2 years** post-transaction; **6 months** post-inquiry; or conspicuously published address). Every commercial electronic message needs **sender identification and a working unsubscribe**. Purely **transactional** messages (e.g., "your viewing is confirmed," receipts) are exempt from the consent requirement but still need ID + unsubscribe formalities. Penalties: **up to $10M per violation (organizations) / $1M (individuals)**. Saved-search alerts the user explicitly configured are the easy case — but capture a distinct marketing consent for anything beyond the alert itself. Sources: https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm ; https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm ; https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/guides/2023/doing-business-in-canada-casl (accessed 2026-08-28).

## 6. Rentals — RTA 2006, ORT, screening, e-signatures

**Risk: MEDIUM (application-fee prohibition is the trap; screening is a phase-2 minefield). What to do: never charge renters to apply; build screening later with counsel-approved consent + criteria list.**

- **Statute/tribunal:** *The Residential Tenancies Act, 2006*, SS 2006, c R-22.0001, administered by the **Office of Residential Tenancies (ORT)** (dispute jurisdiction to **$30,000**). Sources: https://www.canlii.org/en/sk/laws/astat/ss-2006-c-r-22.0001/latest/ss-2006-c-r-22.0001.html ; https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/23011/formats/29464/download (accessed 2026-08-28).
- **Application fees are prohibited:** *"A landlord must not charge a person for accepting an application for a tenancy, processing an application for a tenancy, investigating an applicant's suitability as a tenant, or accepting a person as a tenant."* Source: RTA 2006 via CanLII link above (accessed 2026-08-28). **Platform implication:** charging tenants for applications/screening on landlords' behalf would likely be attacked as doing indirectly what landlords can't do directly — if screening is monetized, charge the **landlord** (and even then, landlord recouping from tenant is barred). **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW.**
- **Security deposits:** capped at **one month's rent** (*"A landlord must not require or accept a security deposit that is greater than the equivalent of one month's rent"*); (SK practice also allows payment split half up-front / half within two months — **UNVERIFIED**, confirm section). Source: RTA 2006, CanLII link above (accessed 2026-08-28). A platform should not hold deposits (see §1, §4).
- **Tenant screening / credit checks:** require the applicant's **signed/express consent** (credit-reporting law + PIPEDA); use only tenancy-relevant criteria. Sources: https://www.singlekey.com/run-credit-check-tenant/ ; https://tenantrights.ca/saskatchewan/what-landlords-can-ask-on-rental-applications-in-saskatchewan (accessed 2026-08-28). Exact SK credit-reporting statute citation **UNVERIFIED** (confirm *The Credit Reporting Act* (SK) requirements incl. any registration of reporting agencies — relevant if Portage itself compiles/report scores).
- **Human-rights limits:** the **Saskatchewan Human Rights Code** prohibits housing discrimination on protected grounds including race, age, family status and **receipt of public assistance**; discriminatory application questions can be complained of to the Saskatchewan Human Rights Commission. Source: https://saskhrc.ca/education-resources/information-sheets/landlords-tenants-and-housing-discrimination/ (accessed 2026-08-28). **Platform duty:** design application forms/AI-scoring so prohibited criteria are neither collected nor inferable (an AI screening tool that proxies protected grounds is a live liability).
- **E-signatures:** *The Electronic Information and Documents Act, 2000*, SS 2000, c E-7.22 gives electronic documents/signatures legal effect for contracts generally; **leases and purchase agreements can generally be e-signed**, while **registration documents at ISC land titles** are governed separately by *The Land Titles Act, 2000* and its execution/witnessing requirements; the 2020 Real Estate Act amendments were expressly aimed at enabling modern/electronic transaction technology for registrants. Sources: https://www.canlii.org/en/sk/laws/stat/ss-2000-c-e-7.22/latest/ss-2000-c-e-7.22.html ; https://www.lawsociety.sk.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/2021-02-09-EID-Regs-QA-for-LSS-1.pdf ; https://www.esignglobal.com/blog/using-docusign-saskatchewan-land-titles-isc-forms-registration ; https://srec.ca/wp-content/uploads/PDF/IB/IB_AmendmentsRealEstateAct.pdf (accessed 2026-08-28). **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW:** confirm EIDA's exclusion list (UECA-style acts typically carve out wills, some land-interest documents requiring registration) before shipping lease e-signing.

## 7. Purchase transactions with no agent — who papers the deal, and the UPL line

**Risk: MEDIUM. What to do: route both parties to independent lawyers early; offer only third-party/education content around forms until counsel blesses a template strategy.**

- **Practice reality in SK private sales:** *"the buyer is responsible to prepare and present the Offer to Purchase"* — lawyers then do the conveyance (title transfer, mortgage payout/registration, tax adjustments) once terms are agreed; Saskatchewan runs a **Torrens** system through **ISC**, where the transfer authorization comes from the registered owner's signature *"verified by affidavit of execution or the signature of a witness who is a practicing lawyer in Saskatchewan."* Sources: https://www.rickcarlson.com/realestate.php?article=private ; https://plea.org/housing/buying-selling/transferring-title ; https://www.saskregistries.ca/landtitles/transfer ; Law Society residential-conveyance checklist: https://www.lawsociety.sk.ca/wp-content/uploads/ResidentialPurchasesSales.pdf (accessed 2026-08-28). Practically, **every Portage sale ends at two law offices** — lean into that (lawyer directory, checklists) rather than replacing it.
- **Unauthorized practice of law:** under **The Legal Profession Act, 1990**, non-members may not practise law or provide legal services to the public; however, Saskatchewan **"explicitly exempted… the provision of legal information"** from the practice of law, with exceptions/exemptions in s.31 and Law Society rules; the Law Society enforces UPL where the public is at risk or misled. Sources: https://www.lawsociety.sk.ca/regulation/definition-of-the-practice-of-law/ ; https://www.lawsociety.sk.ca/wp-content/uploads/faq-practice-of-law-and-exceptions-and-exemptions.pdf ; https://www.justice.gc.ca/eng/rp-pr/jr/ecjh-eamjc/appendix-annexe.html (accessed 2026-08-28). **Where the line likely sits (NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW):** publishing a generic, lawyer-drafted fillable "Offer to Purchase" with plain-language explanations ≈ legal information; **selecting clauses for a user, completing forms for them, or answering "what should I write here?"** ≈ legal services (and likely a "trade" under §1). Any template program should be lawyer-authored, disclaimed, and untouched by platform staff per-transaction.

## 8. AVM & AI features

**Risk: MEDIUM (no AI-specific statute yet; general deceptive-marketing law fully applies and got teeth). What to do: disclaim and version the AVM, human-review AI listing text with owner attestation, and audit all "free/zero-fee/accuracy" claims.**

- **AI-specific law status:** Bill C-27 (CPPA + **AIDA**) **died on the Order Paper when Parliament was prorogued Jan 6, 2025**; **as of mid-2026 no successor federal AI or privacy-reform bill has passed** — governing law is the existing patchwork (PIPEDA, Competition Act, sectoral rules; Québec's Law 25 automated-decision rules don't reach a SK-only product). Sources: https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2025/01/prorogations-digital-impact ; https://www.mcinnescooper.com/publications/the-demise-of-the-artificial-intelligence-and-data-act-aida-5-key-lessons/ ; https://srinstitute.utoronto.ca/news/whats-next-for-aida ; https://d-central.tech/ai-regulation-canada/ (accessed 2026-08-28). ISED's **voluntary generative-AI code of conduct** is the current soft-law benchmark (**adherence optional**).
- **No Canadian rule specifically regulates consumer-facing AVMs** (OSFI's model-risk guideline E-23 binds federally regulated financial institutions, not startups) — the exposure is **misrepresentation**: an AVM number a consumer relies on is a representation by Portage. Mitigations: label as *estimate, not an appraisal or market advice*; show ranges/confidence and refresh dates; keep the model away from individualized "list at $X" advice (also a §1 trading-line issue). (**Researcher synthesis — NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW.**)
- **Competition Act deceptive marketing:** civil provision **s.74.01** bans representations "false or misleading in a material respect"; criminal s.52 covers knowing/reckless cases. Penalties post-2022/2024 amendments are severe — individuals up to the greater of **$750k** ($1M repeat) or **3× benefit**; corporations up to the greater of **$10M** ($15M repeat), 3× benefit, or **3% of annual worldwide gross revenues** (corporate figure per amended s.74.1 — **verify exact current numbers**); **drip pricing** is deemed misleading (only government-imposed charges may be excluded from headline prices — sharpened by Bill C-59, in force June 20, 2024), and **private parties gain expanded access to bring deceptive-marketing cases (June 2025)**. Sources: https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-34/section-74.01-20220623.html ; https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations ; https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2024/07/false-advertising-and-greenwashing-bill-c-59-changes-to-competition-act ; https://www.osler.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/The-amended-Canadian-Competition-Act.pdf (accessed 2026-08-28).
- **AI-generated listing descriptions:** if Portage's model writes the copy, Portage shares authorship of any embellishment ("newly renovated," square footage, "steps to school") — build owner review/edit + a factual-claims attestation, keep generation logs, and contract for owner responsibility, while recognizing consumer-facing disclaimers don't erase s.74.01 exposure. General "general impression" test applies (representation judged by literal meaning **and** general impression). (**Researcher synthesis of cited provisions.**)
- **"Zero-fee" positioning:** if genuinely free to list/buy/rent, fine; the moment any obligatory charge exists anywhere in a flow, headline "free/$0" claims become drip-pricing bait — price transparently.
