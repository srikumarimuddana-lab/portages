## 1. For-sale market: Regina vs Saskatoon vs Saskatchewan

**Regina is in a record-setting seller's market.** Per the Saskatchewan REALTORS Association (SRA) June 2026 release (reported 2026-07): Regina's benchmark price hit **$356,400 in June 2026** — a third consecutive monthly record (May: $350,200), **+5% YoY** ([realestatemagazine.ca, Jul 2026](https://realestatemagazine.ca/saskatchewan-home-sales-surge-in-june-as-inventory-hits-record-lows/); [Global News, Jul 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/11766259/home-prices-record-high-saskatchewan/)). June sales were a **June record of 432 homes (+8% YoY, +23% vs 10-yr average)**, and Regina entered July 2026 with just **1.6 months of supply — roughly half its own 10-year average** (same sources). A balanced market is 4–6 months ([620 CKRM, Jun 2026](https://www.620ckrm.com/2026/06/16/regina-rental-market-rises-as-national-rents-decline-driven-by-migration-and-tight-vacancy-rate/) context reporting).

Other 2026 Regina reference points:
- **April 2026:** average price ~$358,100 (+4% YoY); **347 sales** (−4% YoY but +16% vs 10-yr avg); **457 new listings** (below 10-yr avg) → **SNLR ≈ 76%** (seller's territory; >60% = sellers); **~560 active listings** at month end ([madeinca.ca, 2026](https://madeinca.ca/regina-real-estate-statistics/); [kentbraaten.com, Apr 2026](https://www.kentbraaten.com/blog/regina-real-estate-market-stats-april-2026/)).
- **February 2026:** MLS HPI benchmark $336,400, **+7% YoY** ([wealthnorth.ca](https://wealthnorth.ca/mortgages/housing-market/regina/)).
- **June 2026 (aggregator views):** median sold ~$310K; average sold price $379,675 (+5.6% YoY); DOM 48 days ([searchlistingsonline.ca](https://www.searchlistingsonline.ca/market-report/regina)) vs composite benchmark ~$350K (+3.7%) and **DOM 21 days** ([ovlix.com](https://www.ovlix.com/for-sale/Saskatchewan/Regina/market)). The DOM discrepancy reflects different listing pools/methodologies; the tight-supply SRA data supports the faster figure for well-priced homes.
- **Full-year 2025:** Regina recorded **3,840 sales**, ~2% below 2024's record and ~24% above the 10-year average; December 2025 benchmark $330,900, **+~7% YoY** ([kelseysmith.ca, Dec 2025](https://kelseysmith.ca/blog/regina-real-estate-market-update-december-2025); [peterfourlas.ca, Dec 2025](https://peterfourlas.ca/regina-saskatchewan-housing-market-update-december-2025-%F0%9F%93%8A%F0%9F%8F%A1/)). Implied monthly range: ~150–250 sales in winter, 350–430+ in spring/summer.

**Saskatoon (comparison):** benchmark **$448,400 in June 2026 (record; +~5% YoY)**, 589 June sales (+22% vs 10-yr avg), also **1.6 months of supply** with inventory 43% below the 10-year average — the tightest in the province; July 2026 benchmark eased slightly to **$447,600 (+~4% YoY)** ([realestatemagazine.ca](https://realestatemagazine.ca/saskatchewan-home-sales-surge-in-june-as-inventory-hits-record-lows/); [searchlistingsonline.ca, Jul 2026](https://www.searchlistingsonline.ca/market-report/saskatoon)).

**Saskatchewan overall:** **1,849 sales in June 2026 (+5% YoY, +18% vs 10-yr avg)**; provincial benchmark **$385,900 (June record)**; in **July 2026** CREA reported SK average price **$368,945 (+4.9% YoY — one of the largest provincial gains)** and benchmark **$383,500 (+3.6%)**, with Saskatchewan in "borderline sellers' territory" while the national market is balanced (national avg $674,819, +0.2% YoY; national SNLR 51.3%) ([Global News, Jul 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/11766259/home-prices-record-high-saskatchewan/); [Royal LePage/CREA, Aug 2026](https://blog.royallepage.ca/crea-monthly-housing-report-july-2026/); [CREA release, Aug 18 2026](https://www.crea.ca/media-hub/news/canadian-home-sales-activity-little-changed-in-march-2-2-2-2/)). Full-year **2025: 16,409 provincial sales (+0.8% YoY; second-highest ever)** ([stats.crea.ca](https://stats.crea.ca/mls/sra-residential-activity/)). BNN Bloomberg called Saskatchewan's price growth a national outlier ("needle in a haystack," [Apr 2026](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/economics/2026/04/11/needle-in-a-haystack-saskatchewan-housing-prices-depart-from-national-trend/)).

**Implication for Portage:** homes sell fast at record prices with thin inventory. That's *good* for owner-direct sellers (low marketing risk — a fairly priced Regina home finds buyers quickly, weakening the "you need an agent to sell" argument) but *hard* for a new marketplace's buy-side (few listings to aggregate; buyers cluster where inventory is).

## 2. Rental market: Regina

- **Vacancy:** purpose-built apartment vacancy in Regina CMA held at **2.7% in 2025** (Oct-2025 survey), below its 10-year average; **3-bed+ vacancy fell to 1.4%** (from 3.9%) — family-size units are scarce ([CMHC 2025 Rental Market Report](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/market-reports/rental-market-reports-major-centres), published Dec 2025). Saskatoon: **3.3%** ([SaskToday, 2026](https://www.sasktoday.ca/provincial-news/saskatchewan-rental-market-stabilizing-as-new-housing-boosts-choice-says-industry-group-11883455)). Industry groups describe the market as "easing toward stability" in 2026 as new supply lands ([620 CKRM, Feb 2026](https://www.620ckrm.com/2026/02/15/regina-rental-market-easing-toward-stability-experts-say/)).
- **Rents (asking):** Regina average **$1,403 all property types (Jul 2026)** — among the most affordable large markets in Canada; **1-bed $1,300, 2-bed $1,540** ([Rentals.ca National Rent Report, Aug 2026](https://rentals.ca/national-rent-report)). Zumper corroborates: 1-bed ~$1,290, 2-bed ~$1,530 (Jul 2026, [zumper.com](https://www.zumper.com/rent-research/regina-sk)). Cross-provider April-2026 ranges: overall $1,372–$1,443; 1-bed $1,269–$1,384; 2-bed $1,517–$1,576 ([GoodDoors, Apr 2026](https://www.gooddoors.com/regina-rental-market-update-2026)).
- **Trend:** Regina rents are **rising ~12% over the 13 months to mid-2026 (to ~$1,471 avg)** even as **national asking rents fell 4.0% YoY to $2,037 (22nd straight monthly decline)** — Rentals.ca attributes Regina's divergence to interprovincial migration and tight vacancy ([620 CKRM citing Rentals.ca, Jun 16 2026](https://www.620ckrm.com/2026/06/16/regina-rental-market-rises-as-national-rents-decline-driven-by-migration-and-tight-vacancy-rate/); [Rentals.ca, Aug 2026](https://rentals.ca/national-rent-report)). CMHC likewise recorded 2025 rent growth above the 10-year average despite slowing ([CMHC, Dec 2025](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/market-reports/rental-market-reports-major-centres)).
- **Supply/structure:** Regina's purpose-built stock grew **+2.2% in 2025**, with 3-bed+ units accounting for over half the additions ([CMHC, Dec 2025](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/market-reports/rental-market-reports-major-centres)). Provincially, **6,371 new rental units were completed 2023–2025**, taking Saskatchewan's purpose-built rental stock to a record **44,802 units in 2025** ([Gov of Saskatchewan, Aug 20 2026](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/government/news-and-media/2026/august/20/minister-of-finance-provides-update-on-housing-in-saskatchewan)). Regina's condo (secondary) rental segment stayed tighter than purpose-built, with steady-but-low vacancy ([CMHC, Dec 2025](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/market-reports/rental-market-reports-major-centres)). A related CMHC-derived figure — 2,000+ rental condos added in 2025 pushing condos to 37% of the rental universe at 1.7% vacancy — surfaced in search results but its geographic scope (Regina vs national) is **UNVERIFIED**; treat directionally only. Exact Regina purpose-built universe count (likely ~15K units) and Regina renter-household share (~30%) are **UNVERIFIED** in this pass; city occupied dwellings were 92,129 in 2021 ([StatCan 2021 Census](https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/as-sa/fogs-spg/page.cfm?lang=E&topic=1&dguid=2021A00054706027)).
- **Student demand:** University of Regina hit record enrolment of **17,409 (Fall 2024, +3.1% YoY)** ([uregina.ca, Sep 2024](https://www.uregina.ca/news/2024/university-of-regina-sees-highest-enrolment-in-history-with-a-record-17,409-students-registered-for-fall-term.html)); winter 2024 was a record 16,200 ([Global News, Jan 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10216134/university-of-regina-record-enrolment-winter-2024/)). But **international intake is falling fast** under federal caps: new international undergrads dropped from 958 (winter 2024) to **393 (winter 2025)**; total international UG ~3,644 in winter 2025 ([980 CJME, Apr 2026](https://www.cjme.com/2026/04/08/university-of-regina-navigates-path-forward-amid-sharp-drop-in-international-students/)). Saskatchewan Polytechnic faces a **$14–15M budget shortfall** from the international decline ([620 CKRM, Aug 2025](https://www.620ckrm.com/2025/08/14/immigration-changes-hit-regina-schools-international-intake/)). Sask Polytech Regina-campus headcount: **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Newcomer demand:** the SINP (provincial nominee program) was cut ~50% federally to **3,625 nominations for 2025** (lowest since 2009), then raised by 1,136 to **4,761** in Aug 2025; 75% reserved for people already in SK on temporary status; sweeping 2026 changes announced Dec 2025 ([CIC News, Aug 2025](https://www.cicnews.com/2025/08/saskatchewan-receives-increased-immigration-quota-0859078.html); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/saskatchewan/saskatchewan-immigration-nominee-increase-shortagaes-1.7616630); [CIC News, Dec 2025](https://www.cicnews.com/2025/12/saskatchewan-pnp-announces-nomination-allocation-and-sweeping-changes-for-2026-1263899.html)). Net: newcomer rental demand persists but the firehose of 2023–24 has narrowed — Regina rent growth is currently driven more by interprovincial migration ([620 CKRM, Jun 2026](https://www.620ckrm.com/2026/06/16/regina-rental-market-rises-as-national-rents-decline-driven-by-migration-and-tight-vacancy-rate/)).

## 3. Population & growth

- **Regina CMA population: 282,032 (July 1, 2024 StatCan estimate, +3.91% YoY)** — one of Canada's fastest growth rates that year ([TheDataInsider summary of StatCan, 2024](https://thedatainsider.com/regina-saskatchewan-population-statistics/)). Economic Development Regina reports growth of **+13.1% (+33,819) between 2021 and 2025**, making Regina among Canada's youngest, fastest-growing cities ([EDR, 2025/2026](https://economicdevelopmentregina.com/media-releases/regina-among-canadas-youngest-and-fastest-growing-cities/)) — implying a CMA around **~283K by mid-2025** (2021 CMA base 249,217). StatCan's July-1-2025 subprovincial release (Jan 14, 2026) shows CMA growth nationally slowed to +1.0% ([StatCan Daily](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260114/dq260114a-eng.htm)); Regina-specific 2025 figure not retrievable in this pass (**UNVERIFIED**; city-proper 2026 estimate ~270,480 per [World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/cities/canada/regina), unofficial).
- **Households:** City of Regina had **92,129 occupied private dwellings** at the 2021 census (+5.4% vs 2016), on a city population of 226,404 ([StatCan Census 2021](https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/as-sa/fogs-spg/page.cfm?lang=E&topic=1&dguid=2021A00054706027)); CMA household count is higher (~105K, **UNVERIFIED**). The city is planning for a major population surge in its official community plan update ([620 CKRM, Feb 2026](https://www.620ckrm.com/2026/02/23/city-of-regina-prepares-for-massive-population-surge/)).
- **Construction:** Regina posted **1,687 housing starts in 2025 vs 1,223 in 2024 (+38%)**, credited to rezoning, permit fast-tracking and the Housing Accelerator Fund; builders project 1,300–1,700 for 2026 ([620 CKRM, Jan 29 2026](https://www.620ckrm.com/2026/01/29/regina-housing-starts-jump-nearly-40-in-2025-as-policy-changes-speed-development/)). Saskatchewan starts were **+23.9% YoY in July 2026**, second-fastest growth among provinces ([650 CKOM, Aug 23 2026](https://www.ckom.com/2026/08/23/saskatchewan-housing-starts-up-23-9-per-cent-year-over-year/)); national 2025 starts totalled 259,028 (+5.6%) ([CMHC via Barchart, Jan 2026](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/37082657/cmhc-says-housing-starts-totalled-259-028-in-2025-up-5-6-per-cent-from-2024)).

## 4. How Regina transacts today

**Buy/sell (resale):**
- **Realtor.ca is the national default:** 633M visits, 113M unique visitors, 2B+ listing page views, 3:50 avg session in 2025 ([CREA Café, 2026](https://www.crea.ca/cafe/realtor-ca-highlights-for-2025-a-look-at-the-numbers/)); ~90% of traffic is Canadian ([Similarweb, Mar 2025](https://www.similarweb.com/website/realtor.ca/)). Virtually all of Regina's ~3,800–4,300 annual MLS sales flow through SRA members' MLS → Realtor.ca ([stats.crea.ca/board/sra](https://stats.crea.ca/board/sra/)).
- **Commissions:** Saskatchewan uses tiered structures — commonly **6% on the first $100K / 4% on the next $100K / 2% on balance**, or **7%/3%** ([WOWA SK commission calculator, 2026](https://wowa.ca/calculators/commission-calculator-saskatchewan)); a 2026 study pegs the average effective total at **~4.38% of sale price**, typically split roughly evenly between listing and buyer sides ([rate-my-agent.com, 2026](https://www.rate-my-agent.com/Realtor-commission-Canada)); commissions are negotiable, seller-paid ([rocketadvance.ca](https://rocketadvance.ca/commercial/average-real-estate-commission-rates-in-saskatchewan-explained/)). **On Regina's June-2026 benchmark $356,400: 6/4/2 ⇒ ~$13,128; 7/3 ⇒ ~$14,692; 4.38% flat ⇒ ~$15,610 — i.e., ≈$13,000–$15,600 + tax per sale** (GST applies; SK PST on commissions also applies per industry practice — tax treatment **UNVERIFIED** this pass). That's Portage's headline savings pitch: **~4% of the home's value**.
- **Discount/flat-fee players active in Regina/SK:** **2% Realty** (Regina office, 1708 8th Ave; ~2% total commission, [regina.2percentrealty.ca](https://regina.2percentrealty.ca/); [2 Percent Realty Platinum on SK fees](https://2percentrealtyplatinum.ca/real-estate-fees-in-saskatchewan/)); **EasyList Realty** flat-fee MLS ([easylistrealty.ca](https://www.easylistrealty.ca/mls-listings-sk)); **Realty Hub Saskatchewan** ([realtyhubsk.com](https://www.realtyhubsk.com/)); **PropertyGuys.com** Regina franchise (flat-fee FSBO marketing, no commission — largest private-sale network in North America, [propertyguys.com](https://propertyguys.com/search/ca/sk); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PropertyGuys.com)); plus FSBO classifieds [forsalebyowner.ca](https://www.forsalebyowner.ca/real-estate/canada/regina/SK/homes/for-sale-rent-lease) and [propertysold.ca Regina](https://saskatchewan.propertysold.ca/regina/). ComFree/FairSquare no longer operates (wound down nationally in 2021 — **UNVERIFIED in this pass**, background knowledge). So "cheaper selling" exists, but no zero-fee, tech-first, owner-direct *marketplace* exists locally.
- **Private-sale mechanics are simple in SK:** no land-transfer tax; ISC land-titles transfer fee raised from 0.3% to **0.4% of title value** effective Jul 29, 2023 (~$1,400 on the benchmark home) ([skrealestatelaw.com](https://skrealestatelaw.com/iscpricingchanges/); [Ratehub](https://www.ratehub.ca/land-transfer-tax-saskatchewan)); lawyer fees typically **$1,000–$2,500** per side ([estateagentpower.com](https://estateagentpower.com/blogs/selling/how-much-are-legal-fees-when-selling-property-in-saskatchewan); [skrealestatelaw.com](https://skrealestatelaw.com/how-much-money-do-i-need/)). A private Regina sale can close for <$3,000 all-in vs ~$15K commissioned.

## 5. Rental applications, scams and pain points

- **Scam prevalence is real and rising in SK:** the Financial and Consumer Affairs Authority of Saskatchewan (FCAA), the SRA, the Saskatchewan Landlord Association and BBB Saskatchewan have all warned of increasingly frequent fake-listing scams — scammers impersonate landlords using photos of properties they don't control and demand e-transfer deposits/rent before viewings ([650 CKOM, Oct 2023](https://www.ckom.com/2023/10/10/rental-scams-with-fake-listings-raise-concerns-in-saskatchewan/); [Global News, Aug 2023](https://globalnews.ca/news/9863199/red-flag-rental-housing-scams-rising-saskatchewan)).
- **National scale:** the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre logged **108,878 fraud reports and $638M in reported losses in 2024** ([CAFC 2024 Annual Report](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/annual-reports-2024-rapports-annuels-eng.htm)); cumulative reported losses since 2022 exceed **$2.4B**, and only **5–10% of fraud is ever reported** ([Competition Bureau, Mar 2026](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2026/03/fraud-prevention-month-to-bring-hidden-crime-into-the-spotlight.html); [RCMP Gazette](https://rcmp.ca/en/gazette/cost-fraud-exceeds-financial-loss-victims-say)). Rental-specific CAFC data: **508 rental-fraud victims and $820K+ lost in 2023** ([CBC, Nov 2023](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/thunder-bay/rental-fraud-thunder-bay-1.7007295)); Toronto Police alone took 380+ rental-scam reports in 2024 ([Storeys, 2025](https://storeys.com/experts-rental-scams-tough-to-stop/)); a single Kitchener scheme took 35 victims for $40K ([CBC, 2024](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/kitchener-waterloo/rental-scam-kitchener-1.7327746)). A claim of "$43M rental-fraud losses in 2025" circulates on newcomer blogs — **UNVERIFIED, low-credibility source; do not cite**.
- **Who gets hurt:** newcomers and students renting sight-unseen are prime targets ([moving2canada.com](https://moving2canada.com/living/accommodation/identify-rental-scam-in-canada/)) — exactly Regina's growth demographics (SINP arrivals, U of R students).
- **Dispute landscape:** Saskatchewan's Office of Residential Tenancies (ORT) adjudicates disputes up to **$30,000**; security deposits capped at one month's rent; deposit-return disputes are the classic friction ([Gov of Saskatchewan ORT](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/government/government-structure/boards-commissions-and-agencies/office-of-residential-tenancies); [tenantrights.ca](https://tenantrights.ca/saskatchewan/saskatchewan-damage-deposits-tenant-rights-and-rules)).
- **Portage fit:** verified-owner identity + in-platform chat + booked viewings directly attacks the #1 documented failure mode of Kijiji/Facebook rentals (unverifiable landlords, off-platform e-transfers). This is the strongest wedge the scam data supports.

## 6. Regina economy & affordability — good or bad launch market?

- **Stable, government-anchored economy:** largest employers are the Government of Saskatchewan and Crowns SaskPower, SaskEnergy, SaskTel and SGI; major private employers include EVRAZ steel (~1,700 jobs), Co-op Refinery Complex, Viterra (global grain HQ), Mosaic potash, Brandt, Bayer CropScience, and Farm Credit Canada's national HQ ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/regina); [Farm Credit Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farm_Credit_Canada)). Health care/social assistance, retail and public administration provide 39% of jobs ([IRCC economic profile: Regina](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/campaigns/immigration-matters/local-economies/regina.html)).
- **Labour market:** SK unemployment **6.0% in July 2026** (national 6.5%); **Regina employment +5,400 (+3.5%) YoY** ([Gov of Saskatchewan, Aug 7 2026](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/government/news-and-media/2026/august/07/unemployment-drops-to-60-per-cent-in-july)). Greater Regina 2025 GDP growth was projected at 2.5%, above national, with 10,300 private-sector jobs added ([Economic Development Regina, 2025](https://economicdevelopmentregina.com/corporatepublications/reginas-2025-economic-outlook-stable-growth-with-emerging-challenges/)).
- **Incomes & affordability:** average household income ~$106,100; average salary ~$56,700 ([Point2Homes](https://www.point2homes.com/CA/Demographics/SK/Regina-Demographics.html); [CareerBeacon](https://www.careerbeacon.com/en/cost-of-living/regina_saskatchewan)). RBC ranks Regina **the most affordable housing market it tracks in Canada: ownership costs = 27.2% of median income in Q1 2026 vs 53% nationally**, and the only market below its own long-run average in early 2025 ([RBC Economics, Jul 2026](https://www.rbc.com/en/thought-leadership/economics/canadianhousing/housing-affordability/easing-homeownership-costs-could-soon-slow-in-canada/); [CMT, Jul 2026](https://www.canadianmortgagetrends.com/2026/07/housing-affordability-improves-but-rbc-says-relief-may-be-running-its-course/)). A ~$92K household income carries the average detached home ([WealthNorth, Feb 2026](https://wealthnorth.ca/mortgages/housing-market/regina/)).
- **Why this cuts both ways for Portage:** *For:* on a $356K home, $14K commission is ~25% of the median *annual salary* — the savings story lands harder in a cheap market than in Toronto in relative terms; population growth, first-time-buyer depth, and DIY prairie culture (strong existing FSBO/2%-realty penetration) favour disruption; a compact metro (~283K) is cheap to saturate with marketing. *Against:* absolute commission dollars (and thus absolute savings) are the lowest of any major Canadian city; total fee pool is small (~4,000 sales × ~$14K ≈ **$56M/yr commission pool in Regina**); a seller's market means sellers feel little pain ("my agent sold it in a week"), and buyers — who'd love more inventory — aren't the ones paying fees.

**Total ≈ 10,000–13,000 housing transactions/yr in Regina (sales + leases).**

**Realistic year-1/2 capture:**
- **Rentals (the wedge):** listings are free everywhere already, so Portage competes on trust + tooling, not price. Capturing **3–8% of lease flow in year 1 (≈200–700 leases)** requires onboarding ~30–60 small landlords/PMs plus scraping/syndicating; **year 2 at 10–20% (≈800–1,800 leases)** is plausible if student (Sept/Jan) and newcomer channels convert. Landlord acquisition is the constraint, not renter demand (renters multi-home across platforms).
- **Sales:** year 1 owner-direct listings of **50–150** (mostly converted FSBO-intent sellers), closing **~30–80 sales (0.7–2% of the market)**; year 2 **~100–250 (2.5–6%)** if early "saved $14K in a week" stories are marketable. In a 1.6-months-supply market a fairly priced owner-direct listing *will* sell — the real risks are (a) buyer-agent steering away from non-MLS listings and (b) sellers defaulting to agents out of habit, not economics.
- **Liquidity strategy implications:** (1) launch demand-side value that works with *zero* proprietary supply — AI chat search, AVM and neighbourhood scores over aggregated public listings; (2) treat rentals as the frequency engine (10× the transaction count of FSBO sales) and verified-owner status as the anti-scam differentiator vs Kijiji/Facebook; (3) seed sale-side supply via the existing FSBO/flat-fee ecosystem and builders (1,687 starts) rather than fighting MLS head-on; (4) time pushes to the two rental surges (Sept university intake, spring move season) and the spring listing season; (5) home-services marketplace monetizes every user regardless of transaction capture. Note: whether an unlicensed marketplace can facilitate offers/bookings without triggering Saskatchewan real-estate licensing (Real Estate Act / FCAA oversight) was **not researched — flag for legal review**.

## Key takeaways for a Regina-first launch

1. **Regina is a record-hot seller's market** — benchmark $356,400 (June 2026, +5% YoY), 1.6 months of supply, ~50% below the 10-year average ([SRA via realestatemagazine.ca](https://realestatemagazine.ca/saskatchewan-home-sales-surge-in-june-as-inventory-hits-record-lows/)) — so owner-direct sellers face low risk of "failing to sell," the classic FSBO objection.
2. **But the same tightness starves a new marketplace of inventory**: only ~560 active MLS listings (Apr 2026, [madeinca.ca](https://madeinca.ca/regina-real-estate-statistics/)) and ~76% SNLR — buy-side liquidity must come from aggregation, rentals and new construction, not organic listings alone.
3. **The commission prize is ~$13K–$15.6K per average sale (~4–4.4%)** ([WOWA](https://wowa.ca/calculators/commission-calculator-saskatchewan); [rate-my-agent](https://www.rate-my-agent.com/Realtor-commission-Saskatchewan)) — a ~$56M/yr commission pool in Regina; meaningful per-household, small in aggregate → plan multi-city economics from day 1.
4. **Discount incumbents (2% Realty, EasyList, PropertyGuys Regina) prove local price sensitivity** but none offers a zero-fee, AI-native, owner-direct marketplace — Portage's positioning gap is real ([regina.2percentrealty.ca](https://regina.2percentrealty.ca/); [propertyguys.com](https://propertyguys.com/search/ca/sk)).
5. **Private sales are legally cheap and simple in SK**: no land-transfer tax, ~0.4% ISC title fee, $1,000–$2,500 lawyer fees ([Ratehub](https://www.ratehub.ca/land-transfer-tax-saskatchewan); [skrealestatelaw.com](https://skrealestatelaw.com/iscpricingchanges/)) — an all-in <$3K close vs ~$15K commissioned is a clean, quantifiable pitch.
6. **Rentals are the liquidity wedge**: est. 6,000–9,000 leases/yr vs ~4,000 sales; Kijiji (~400 live ads) and un-verified Facebook groups dominate the informal segment — exactly where scams live ([Kijiji, Aug 2026](https://www.kijiji.ca/b-apartments-condos/regina-area/c37l1700194); [650 CKOM](https://www.ckom.com/2023/10/10/rental-scams-with-fake-listings-raise-concerns-in-saskatchewan/)).
7. **Verified-owner identity is the killer feature the market has documented demand for**: FCAA/SRA/BBB scam warnings, 508 CAFC rental-fraud victims ($820K+) in 2023, 5–10% reporting rates ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/thunder-bay/rental-fraud-thunder-bay-1.7007295); [Competition Bureau](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2026/03/fraud-prevention-month-to-bring-hidden-crime-into-the-spotlight.html)) — market "verified owners, zero scams" harder than "zero fees" on the rental side.
8. **Regina rents are rising against a falling national market** (+~12% over 13 months to ~$1,471 while Canada fell 4% YoY) with 2.7% vacancy ([620 CKRM/Rentals.ca, Jun 2026](https://www.620ckrm.com/2026/06/16/regina-rental-market-rises-as-national-rents-decline-driven-by-migration-and-tight-vacancy-rate/); [CMHC](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/market-reports/rental-market-reports-major-centres)) — landlords have pricing power and motivation to list widely; renters are desperate for better search.
9. **Demand tailwinds are strong but shifting**: CMA grew ~3.9% in 2023-24 and +13.1% since 2021 ([StatCan via TheDataInsider](https://thedatainsider.com/regina-saskatchewan-population-statistics/); [EDR](https://economicdevelopmentregina.com/media-releases/regina-among-canadas-youngest-and-fastest-growing-cities/)), but international-student intake is down sharply (958→393 new intl UGs at U of R) and SINP is capped at 4,761 ([CJME](https://www.cjme.com/2026/04/08/university-of-regina-navigates-path-forward-amid-sharp-drop-in-international-students/); [CIC News](https://www.cicnews.com/2025/08/saskatchewan-receives-increased-immigration-quota-0859078.html)) — don't over-index the newcomer channel; interprovincial migrants matter more now.
10. **Realtor.ca's gravity is enormous** (633M visits in 2025; [CREA Café](https://www.crea.ca/cafe/realtor-ca-highlights-for-2025-a-look-at-the-numbers/)) — Portage should not fight it for search intent head-on in year 1; win on rentals, FSBO, AI tools, and neighbourhood/AVM content SEO where Realtor.ca is weak.
11. **Economics of the city favour a low-cost product**: most affordable major market in Canada (RBC 27.2% vs 53% national, Q1 2026), stable government employment, 6.0% unemployment ([RBC](https://www.rbc.com/en/thought-leadership/economics/canadianhousing/housing-affordability/easing-homeownership-costs-could-soon-slow-in-canada/); [Gov of SK](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/government/news-and-media/2026/august/07/unemployment-drops-to-60-per-cent-in-july)) — a value-conscious, first-time-buyer-heavy population is the right early-adopter profile.
12. **Realistic year-1 scoreboard**: ~200–700 leases + ~30–80 owner-direct sales ≈ 2–6% of Regina's ~10–13K annual housing transactions; year-2 target 2–3× that. Success metric should be *supply-side share* (listings, verified owners) and rental liquidity first; sales GMV follows brand trust.
13. **Watch items / gaps to close**: SRA's official July/August 2026 Regina print (DOM, SNLR) once accessible; CMHC Regina universe + turnover tables; Facebook group member counts (need logged-in check); SK licensing/regulatory perimeter for an unlicensed marketplace facilitating transactions; PST treatment of commissions for the savings calculator.
