## Part 1 — The honest business-model problem

Your design says **"FREE FOR EVERYONE — No listing fees · No commission · No paywalls"** and, for service pros, **"no subscription."** That is currently **zero revenue surface**.

This matters because the research produced one unambiguous pattern:

> Every Canadian company that attacked commissions with fixed or falling revenue per listing, against cyclical volume, carrying human costs — **died**. FairSquare (Desjardins, $60.5M) shut down in Feb 2023. Properly sold for parts. Unreserved paused after a $33.85M seed. Homie (US, $35M) was gone by 2024.

And in the US, **Azibo** — free, planning to monetize via banking — was absorbed by TurboTenant in May 2025. **Free with no plan is equally fatal.**

### What your scope cut removed

Your decision to drop lease generation and rent collection was **regulatorily correct** (see Part 3), but it removed the two highest-yield rental revenue lines from the US playbook — tenant-paid applications and payment spread. And Saskatchewan law independently blocks the first one anyway:

> *"A landlord must not charge a person for accepting an application for a tenancy, processing an application for a tenancy, investigating an applicant's suitability as a tenant, or accepting a person as a tenant."* — The Residential Tenancies Act, 2006 (SK)

**So the money has to come from somewhere else.** Here is the realistic menu, all compatible with "free to list, free to search, free to message."

**Golden rule from the research:** price every add-on **loudly and up front**. Canada's Competition Act now deems **drip pricing** misleading (Bill C-59, in force June 2024), with corporate penalties up to the greater of $10M, 3× benefit, or **3% of worldwide gross revenue** — and since June 2025 private parties have expanded rights to bring deceptive-marketing cases. *"Zero-fee" brand equity dies on the first hidden fee.*

## Part 2 — Regina go-to-market

### Why Regina works
- **Most affordable market RBC tracks** (27.2% of income vs 53% nationally) — value-conscious, first-time-buyer-heavy early adopters
- **~$14,000 commission on a $356,400 home ≈ 25% of the median annual salary** — the savings story lands hard
- **Private sales are cheap and simple in SK**: no land-transfer tax, 0.4% ISC title fee, $1,000–$2,500 lawyer → **<$3,000 all-in vs ~$15,000 commissioned**
- **Weak direct tech competition**: HouseSigma absent, liv.rent absent, Point2 exited for-sale in 2024
- **Compact metro (~283k)** is cheap to saturate with marketing
- **Existing price sensitivity is proven** — 2% Realty, EasyList, Realty Hub and a PropertyGuys franchise all operate in Regina

### Why Regina is hard
- **1.6 months of supply** — sellers feel no pain ("my agent sold it in a week"), and there is little inventory for a new marketplace to aggregate
- **Small absolute pool**: ~$56M/yr total commission in Regina. Plan multi-city economics from day one
- **Realtor.ca's gravity is enormous** — 633M visits, 113M unique visitors in 2025. Do not fight it for search intent in year 1
- **Student/newcomer inflow is narrowing**: U of R new international undergrads fell 958 → 393 (winter 2024 → winter 2025); SINP nominations capped at 4,761. Interprovincial migration now drives Regina rent growth more than newcomers

### The sequence

**Phase 0 — Rentals first (months 0–6).**
Rentals are **~6,000–9,000 lease-ups/yr vs ~4,000 sales** — roughly 2× the volume, far higher frequency, and naturally owner-direct. Attack the free tier (Kijiji + Facebook) on **verification**, not price. Target: 30–60 small landlords and property managers onboarded; 200–700 leases in year 1.

**Phase 1 — FSBO sellers (months 4–12).**
Convert existing FSBO-intent sellers (PropertyGuys, forsalebyowner.ca, saskhouses.com users). Target 50–150 listings, 30–80 closings. Every "saved $14,000 in nine days" story is your marketing.

**Phase 2 — Services + directories (months 6–18).**
Turn the audience into revenue via E1–E5, F2, F3.

**Phase 3 — Second city (month 18+).**
Saskatoon is the obvious next step (benchmark $448,400, same provincial MLS, same regulator, 1.6 months supply).

### Demand-side value with zero proprietary supply
The chicken-and-egg killer. Launch with things that work before you have listings: AI chat search over aggregated public listings, the Regina AVM, neighbourhood scores from open data, and the quarterly **Regina Rent & Price Index** (C10) for free press and SEO.

### Timing
Push hard at the two rental surges — **September (U of R intake)** and the **spring move season** — and the spring listing season for sales.

## Part 3 — Regulatory reality (research, not legal advice)

### The core question: do you need a real-estate licence?

Saskatchewan's **Real Estate Act** (SS 1995, c R-1.3), administered by **SREC** under the **FCAA**, defines "trade" broadly — including *"an offering, advertisement, listing or showing of real estate."* But the registration trigger is trading **for another person and for compensation**.

**Section 3(1) exemption:** *"this Act does not apply to: (a) a trade in real estate by an owner of the real estate or a person who has a material interest in the real estate…"*

**The structure that keeps you outside it:** the **owner** is the exempt trader; Portage sells advertising space and software. This is how Kijiji and Facebook Marketplace operate without brokerage licences — and it is the model **DuProprio successfully defended in court**:

- Acquitted of six charges (Court of Québec, Nov 2011), upheld by Québec Superior Court (July 2013)
- **April 2020**: the Superior Court again held DuProprio *"does not act as an intermediary"* in transactions

⚠️ **Caveat:** those cases construe *Quebec's* statute, and **no express "classifieds/publisher" exemption was located in the Saskatchewan Act or Regulations**. This needs a Saskatchewan legal opinion mapping each feature to s.2/s.3. SREC actively publishes discipline orders — this is a policed regime.

**The alternative architecture:** Bōde **is** a licensed brokerage (RECA-regulated in Alberta), which is how it puts self-represented sellers on MLS® and runs offer flows. **Pick one lane — pure media, or licensed brokerage. Not a hybrid.**

### What crosses the line (needs legal review)

| Activity | Risk |
|---|---|
| Automated AVM shown to everyone, clearly disclaimed | Lower risk |
| **Bespoke "you should list at $X" advice to a specific seller** | Looks like a trading service |
| **Drafting/transmitting offers, negotiating, advising on counters** | Trading **and** unauthorized practice of law |
| **Holding deposits** | No lawful home for the money; SREC + FINTRAC exposure |
| Self-serve viewing calendar the owner controls | Tool-like — defensible |
| **Staff scheduling or conducting showings on a seller's behalf** | "Showing of real estate" — a trade limb |
| **Collecting rent / managing rentals for landlords** | Property management is registrable activity in SK |

### The rest of the compliance map

| Area | Key point |
|---|---|
| **CREA trademarks** | Never use REALTOR®, MLS® or Multiple Listing Service® in branding, product names or SEO. "The MLS® mark can never be used as part of a business name" |
| **MLS data** | CREA's DDF distributes listings only at members' direction, and technology providers need a Data Access Agreement. **A non-member platform cannot get a feed.** Scraping Realtor.ca breaches its terms |
| **FINTRAC** | **No obligations for a pure listings platform** (you are not provincially authorized to act as agent, and not a developer). This changes the day you touch money |
| **PIPEDA** | Saskatchewan has no private-sector privacy statute, so PIPEDA governs. **ID documents are the crown-jewel risk** — the OPC advises copying government ID "should not be a standard operating practice," and has found indefinite retention of ID data violates PIPEDA. Use **verify-then-delete** (store pass/fail + audit hash) |
| **CASL** | Express opt-in (unchecked boxes) for saved-search alerts and marketing; sender ID + working unsubscribe in every message; log consents. **Penalties up to $10M per violation** |
| **Rentals (RTA 2006)** | No application/screening charges to tenants. Security deposits capped at one month's rent. ORT adjudicates to $30,000 |
| **Human rights** | The Saskatchewan Human Rights Code prohibits housing discrimination including on **receipt of public assistance** — design forms and any AI scoring so protected grounds are neither collected nor inferable |
| **E-signatures** | The Electronic Information and Documents Act, 2000 gives e-signatures legal effect generally, but ISC land-titles registration has its own execution/witnessing rules |
| **UPL** | SK explicitly exempts *"the provision of legal information"* from the practice of law. Generic lawyer-drafted templates with plain-language explanation ≈ information. **Completing forms for a user ≈ legal services** |
| **AI/AVM** | Bill C-27 (incl. AIDA) **died on the Order Paper Jan 2025**; no successor passed as of mid-2026. Governing law is the existing patchwork. Label AVMs "estimate, not an appraisal"; require owner review of AI-drafted descriptions |
| **Home services** | "Vetted & insured" is a material representation. Keep a per-pro evidence file (licence check, insurance certificate with expiry, re-verification cadence). SK: TSASK licenses electrical/gas contractors ($10,000 surety bond); several trades are compulsory-apprenticeship |
| **Tax & municipal** | GST registration past $30,000 revenue; **SK PST marketplace-facilitator rules need an accountant's ruling** (services to real property have been PST-taxable since 2017); confirm City of Regina business licence/zoning |
| **CRA platform reporting** | Digital-platform operator rules (first filings Jan 2025) apply to platforms facilitating rentals or paid services. A pure listings site is generally out of scope; paid home-services facilitation likely triggers it |
| **Insurance** | Tech E&O **including media liability** (inaccurate listing content), cyber/privacy, CGL, D&O |

### The tailwind
The **Competition Bureau** obtained a Federal Court order in **Oct 2024** compelling CREA records, investigating commission rules and the REALTOR® Cooperation Policy — expressly probing whether the policy *"makes it more difficult for alternative listing services to compete."* A second order followed for Quebec (May 2025), and in **January 2026** the investigation expanded to **Greater Vancouver REALTORS®**. Separately, RE/MAX settled a commission class action (~$7.8M, Federal Court approval Oct 2025).

**You are precisely the "alternative listing service" the Bureau is worried is being foreclosed.** Worth a submission if consultations reopen.

### Your strategic defence file
Document from day one that Portage (a) charges no commission tied to a transaction, (b) provides software and advertising space, and (c) leaves negotiation, contracting and conveyance to owners and their lawyers. **This record is what won DuProprio its cases.**
